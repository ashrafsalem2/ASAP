# Where somebody works

Where an employee works is kept as a history of dated assignments, not as a column on their record. That distinction is the reason branch costs can be trusted.

A column holds one answer: where they are now. Payroll reading it would charge the whole month to wherever they happened to be on payday. Somebody who transferred on the sixteenth would cost their old branch nothing and their new one a full month — every time anybody moved, in the shop's favour or against it, and nobody would notice because the company total is right either way.

## What the history has to satisfy

Two assignments may not overlap. A day covered twice is a day charged to two branches, so the wage is paid once and reported twice.

A gap is a warning rather than a refusal. Days with no branch are worked and paid for and no branch carries them; that is sometimes deliberate — someone seconded to head office between postings — and sometimes an oversight. The message says which days.

## Transferring

A transfer names the new branch and the first day there. The assignment somebody is on closes the day before, whatever it said before: the transfer is the thing that closes it.

Record it when it happens. A transfer recorded a fortnight late moves real money to the wrong shop, quietly and correctly totalled, so nothing ever looks wrong.
