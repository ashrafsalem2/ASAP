# Entitlement provisions

A provision is what the company owes people for work already done, carried in the accounts before anybody asks for it. Two of them come out of employing staff: end of service, and leave earned and not taken. Both are real liabilities on the day the work is done, not costs that appear on the day somebody resigns or books a holiday.

## Two provisions, two writers

They are posted by different things, on purpose.

**End of service is charged by the payroll run.** Every run works out what that month added to what each person will be owed when they go, splits it across the branches the days were worked at, and posts it with the wages. This is why a branch's staff cost includes it: the shop that had somebody for the month carries what that month cost, including the part nobody has been paid yet.

**Unused leave is posted by a provision run.** Nothing else touches it, because leave moves on its own rhythm — earned by the day, spent when somebody takes it, and revalued whenever a wage changes.

Running both from one place would book end of service twice. If a figure looks doubled, this is the first thing to check.

## What a provision run posts

It computes what the company owes in unused leave today and posts however much that has moved since the last run. Not the whole figure over again — the difference.

That makes the run safe to repeat. Run it monthly, run it twice in a day, run it the morning after a wage rise: each run posts only what changed, and a run with nothing to report posts nothing at all and says so. "Nothing has changed" is an outcome, not a failure.

The figure it measures against is kept per company, alongside the transaction it was last posted under, so you can always trace a balance back to the run that put it there.

## When it says nothing has moved

Somebody earned a day and somebody else took one, and they cancelled out; or the run has already been done today. Neither needs fixing. The ledger already carries the figure this run computed.

## When it refuses

A provision needs two accounts: the expense it is charged to, and the liability it is carried in. Both must be set before anything is posted — half a posting is not a posting, and a run that posted one side would leave the ledger out of balance in a way that is found weeks later.

Set them in [human resources setup](setup.md). The refusal names the exact setting that is empty.

## What is still to come

Payroll's end-of-service charge accumulates month by month, and nothing yet compares that running total against what the entitlement formula says the liability should be. In practice the two drift apart slowly — a wage rise revalues past service, and only the formula knows it. Until the reconciliation is built, the entitlements report is the authority on what the company actually owes, and the ledger balance is the sum of what payroll has charged.
