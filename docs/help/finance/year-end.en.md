# Closing a financial year

Income statement accounts measure a period. They start each one at nothing, and the year-end transfer is what makes that true.

## What it does

On the last day of the year it posts the exact opposite of every balance the income statement accounts hold, and sends the other side — the year's result — to retained earnings, where it belongs to the owners. Without it the new year opens with last year's revenue still on the books and every income statement afterwards is the sum of every year so far.

Each account is cleared per branch, so no shop is left holding a balance the company total says is zero.

Retained earnings takes the result whole. What the owners have is not divisible between shops, and a branch that closes does not take a share of the accumulated profit away with it.

## Before you run it

You need a retained earnings account set up. It is an equity account: what the company earned belongs to its owners.

Earlier years must already have been closed. Closing 2026 while 2025 is still untransferred would sweep up 2025's result and report it as part of 2026 — wrong twice, and with nothing on either statement to say so.

## Afterwards

The year is locked, and so are its periods. It cannot be run twice: there is no version of doing that which is right, so there is nothing for a permission to unlock. If the result itself turns out to be wrong, correct it with an entry in the current year.
