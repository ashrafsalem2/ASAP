# Number series

Every document number in ASAP comes out of a series. A series has lines, each applying from a date, and each holding a pattern for the numbers it issues.

## The line that matters is next year's

A series whose last line ends in December stops the shop trading on the first of January. Adding the line takes ten seconds; finding out you needed it takes a morning, on the busiest day of the year. The screen leads with how many numbers each line has left for exactly that reason.

## Gaps

Whether a series may have gaps is the setting worth thinking about. A gapless series never lets a number be taken and abandoned — the number is issued at the moment the document is committed, which is slower and is what a simplified tax invoice sequence requires. A sequence with holes in it is a question from the authority.

A series that allows gaps hands the number out earlier and does not care if the document is never finished. Right for an internal order number and wrong for an invoice.

## Date order

A series can require that numbers are issued in date order, so document 100 is never dated before document 99. Reasonable for invoices, and awkward for anything back-dated as a matter of course. Somebody with the override permission can post out of order, with a reason.

## What is never rewritten

A line that has issued numbers keeps the first number it started from. It is the record of which numbers are gone, and a series that forgot it would hand the same invoice number out twice.
