# Purchase invoices

A vendor invoice is what you have been asked to pay. Posting it clears the accrual raised when the goods were received and puts the amount on the vendor's ledger.

## Three-way matching

The order says what was agreed, the receipt says what arrived, the invoice says what is charged. Where the invoice price differs from the order price, the difference posts as a purchase price variance rather than being absorbed into the cost of the goods. That is what makes it visible: a supplier who quietly raises prices shows up in one account rather than in a margin that drifted.

## Invoicing what has not arrived

Allowed, and the accrual works the other way. What must not happen is paying twice for one delivery, so the receipt and the invoice each record what they have matched.

## Where it is reported

The invoice posts at the branch of the location the goods were received into. A price variance belongs to the shop that bought at the wrong price, not to head office for having processed the paperwork.

## Corrections

Never edited. A vendor credit memo reverses what needs reversing, and both stay on the vendor's ledger.
