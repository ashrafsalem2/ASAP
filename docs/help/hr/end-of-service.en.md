# End of service

What an employee will be owed when they leave is a liability the company carries now, not a cost that appears on the day somebody resigns. A company that only recognises it then reports a profit every year that it does not have, and then meets the whole bill in the year it finally counts.

## How the award is worked out

Half a month's wage for each of the first five years, and a full month for each year after that. The bands are cumulative, like tax bands: reaching five years does not revalue the first five at the higher rate. Part years count in proportion.

The wage used is the total wage — basic and allowances — not the basic alone, which is what the Labour Law says and what most disputes are about.

## Resignation is worth less

Somebody who resigns keeps a fraction of the award, and the fraction rises with service: nothing under two years, a third between two and five, two thirds between five and ten, and the whole award after ten. Termination, retirement, death and disability carry the full award.

This is why a leaving date with no reason is refused. The reason is an input to the figure, not a note about it.

## The provision

Payroll charges the increase each month, so the liability grows as it is earned and the cost lands in the month the work was done. The charge is split across branches the same way the wage is: an award is earned by working the days, so it belongs where the days were worked.

The bands are data rather than code. Another jurisdiction is a different policy, not a different build.
