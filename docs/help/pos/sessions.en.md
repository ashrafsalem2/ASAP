# Till sessions

A session is one drawer, from the moment it is opened with a float to the moment it is counted and closed.

## Opening

A session names the till and the opening float — the cash put in the drawer before trading. One till, one open session: a drawer cannot be counted twice, so it cannot be worked by two sessions at once.

## The X reading

An X reading is what the drawer should hold right now, without closing anything. Take one before a shift change or whenever somebody wants to check. It changes nothing.

## Closing, and the count

Closing needs the cash actually counted. The difference between that and what the drawer should hold is the till difference, and it is posted rather than argued about: the drawer is right and the ledger is wrong, so the ledger moves. Refusing to post a difference would leave the cash account describing money that is not in the building, which is worse than recording that somebody miscounted.

The difference is charged to the till's own branch. Charged centrally it becomes a single company-wide number nobody owns, which is the state in which small differences stay small differences for years.

## Card takings are not in the drawer

What was taken on cards is excluded from what the drawer should hold. Including it is the mistake that otherwise leaves every till short by the day's card sales, every day, and teaches everybody to ignore the figure.

## Parked sales

A session with parked sales still open warns on closing. A parked sale is a customer who walked away from the counter; leaving one behind at the end of the day means somebody's shopping is still on the system and nobody's till is short.
