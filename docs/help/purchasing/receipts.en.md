# Goods receipts

A receipt is stock arriving. It moves the goods and their cost, and it is the moment an estimate can become a fact.

## What it posts

Stock in at the order price, and an accrual: goods received and not yet invoiced. The accrual is what keeps the balance sheet honest between the lorry arriving and the invoice being keyed — the goods are yours and the money is owed, whatever the paperwork has caught up with.

## Receiving is not invoicing

They can happen in either order and are compared rather than assumed to match. Where the invoice comes in at a different price, the difference is a purchase price variance and posts as one; it does not quietly change what the goods cost.

## Cost settlement

Where the goods had already been sold before they were received — a shop trading on stock the system did not know it had — the sale left an estimated cost. Receiving is one of the events that lets the settlement routine work out the true cost and post the difference. See the inventory topics.

## Partial and over-receipt

An order can be received in parts. Receiving more than was ordered is refused: the difference is a conversation with the supplier rather than something to absorb.
