# Purchase orders

An order is what you have agreed to buy. It commits nothing to the ledger; it is the document the warehouse and the accounts department both work against.

## Quantity and price

The order records what was agreed. The receipt records what arrived and the invoice records what was charged, and the three are compared rather than assumed to match. That comparison is the point of having all three: a delivery short by two, or an invoice at a price nobody agreed, is visible instead of paid.

## Receiving against an order

Goods arriving are matched to order lines. Receiving more than was ordered is refused — the difference is a decision to take on the order, not something to absorb in the warehouse. Receiving less leaves the balance outstanding.

## Approval

Where the company sets a limit, an order above it needs somebody else to approve it. That is a separate permission from raising one, because the whole point is that the two are different people.

## Location

An order names where the goods will be received. That decides which branch the purchase — and any price variance on it — is reported against.
