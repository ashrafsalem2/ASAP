# Financial years and periods

A financial year is divided into periods, usually months. Both can be closed, and closing means nothing may be posted into them again.

## Close in order, and only when you mean it

A period is closed once its figures are agreed. A year is closed once its statements have been issued. Neither is a routine tidying-up: a closed year is refused to everybody, including the owner of the installation, because the accounts have been filed and a late entry would make the filed figures wrong with nothing on the face of them to say so.

A correction to a closed year is a prior-period adjustment and belongs in the current year's books. That is not a workaround; it is what the accounting convention says, and the reason it says it is that last year's statements have been read by people who acted on them.

## Two flags, not one

A year being *closed* stops posting. A year's income having been *transferred* is the entry that moves the result to retained earnings. They are separate on purpose, and running them in the wrong order is the one mistake worth guarding: a year locked before its transfer leaves the result stranded in the income statement accounts with no way to post it out.

The year-end routine does both, in the right order. See the year-end topic.
