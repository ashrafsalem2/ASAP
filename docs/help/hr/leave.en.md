# Leave

Leave earned was never the hard half. What is *left* needs a record of what was taken, and that record is the leave register.

## Only granted leave counts

A request that has merely been made is a question, not an absence. Nothing comes off a balance or a wage until it is granted — otherwise somebody could exhaust their entitlement by asking for it.

Withdrawal stays available after granting. Somebody who was given the week off and came in anyway should not be recorded as having taken it, and editing the dates of a decision they were told about loses the fact that it was ever made.

## Days are calendar days

Counted from one date to the other inclusive, which is how the Labour Law counts leave and the only count that can be checked against a calendar. A working-day count would need the rota of the branch they were at on each of those days, and would change silently if that rota were ever edited.

## What each kind is paid at

Annual leave is paid in full and comes off the balance. Nothing else touches the balance: a system that let sickness eat somebody's holiday would be wrong, and here it would be unlawful.

Sick leave is article 117: the first thirty days at full pay, the next sixty at three quarters, the thirty after that at nothing. The count runs across the leave year rather than restarting at each absence, so ten absences of nine days cost exactly what one absence of ninety costs.

Maternity is ten weeks; marriage and bereavement five days each; Hajj up to fifteen days once in a period of service. Unpaid leave is what it says.

Payroll deducts whatever carries no pay, as a deduction rather than by docking the days worked — both reach the same net figure, and only one leaves a payslip that says what happened.
