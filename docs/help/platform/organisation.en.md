# Companies and branches

A company is a set of books. A branch is a place the company trades from.

## What a company settles at birth

The base currency and the month its financial year opens in. Both are settled the moment anything is posted, because both describe how the existing figures were measured. Changing either afterwards leaves the history meaning something it never did, so the system refuses and the screen greys them.

Everything else about a company — its name, its registrations, whether it is active — can change at any time.

## Why branches matter more than they look

Every document is posted somewhere, and where decides which shop carries its revenue and its cost. A sale posts at the till that rang it up; a wage at the shop the days were worked at; a stock movement at the location the goods moved in. Get that wrong and the branch performance report is quietly describing the wrong shops, and the company total is right either way so nothing looks broken.

## Closing a branch

A branch that closes is made inactive, never removed. Last year's documents point at it and a branch report for last year has to be able to name it.

What is refused is closing the last open one. Every document is posted somewhere, and a company with nowhere to post from cannot trade at all.

## Opening one

Opening a branch is not only an address. A shop needs a location for its stock and a till bound to it before it can sell, and receipt numbering per branch is created automatically so two shops selling at once cannot collide.
