# Stock counts

Everything else in the system records what somebody said happened. A count is the only thing that goes and looks.

That makes it the only real check on all the rest, and the only place a shortfall the system never saw — theft, breakage, a delivery signed for and never put away, a sale keyed twice — becomes a number anybody can act on.

## The sheet is frozen

When a count is started, the sheet records what the system said at that moment. The comparison is against that figure, not against a live one.

Otherwise a sale rung up while somebody walks the aisles becomes a discrepancy nobody can explain, the counters are blamed for it, and within two counts nobody trusts the exercise. The sheet says when it was taken, so anybody arguing with a line knows how old the figure is.

## Nought and not counted are different

A line at nought is a shelf somebody looked at and found empty. A line not counted is a shelf nobody reached.

Posting the first writes the stock off, which is right. Posting the second would write off everything the counters ran out of time for, which is a disaster that looks exactly like diligence. So a count with uncounted lines is refused, and somebody with the override permission can post anyway with a reason — the uncounted lines are left exactly as they were.

## Counting part of a shop

The ordinary case. Nobody counts a supermarket in one evening: you count the aisle, the freezer, the high-value cabinet. Ask for the items you mean and the sheet holds only those.

Every item the location has ever held goes on a full sheet, including the ones the system says are at nothing — otherwise the count cannot find the box nobody ever booked in, which is one of the two things it exists to find. An item that was never at that location can be added while counting, and the whole of it reads as a gain.

## What posting does

Only the differences move. A line that matched needs no movement, and posting one would put a row on every item's history saying nothing happened to it.

The differences post as positive and negative adjustments at the count date. Where a count finds less than the system had, and the company does not allow negative stock, the ordinary refusal applies: the count is telling you the stock was already gone.

## Two permissions

Counting and posting what a count found are separate on purpose. A count writes stock off, and the person holding the clipboard is not usually the person who should decide that what they could not find has gone.

## Correcting a count

A posted count is never edited. Count again: the second count is the honest way to correct the first, and both stay on the record where somebody can see what changed between them.
