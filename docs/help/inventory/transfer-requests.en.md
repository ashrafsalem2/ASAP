# Transfer requests

A transfer request is a branch asking for stock from somewhere that has it. Somebody at the place holding the stock answers, and what they agree to becomes a transfer.

It works exactly like a purchase requisition, for the same reason. A branch cannot help itself to a warehouse's stock any more than a department can help itself to the company's money. The place holding the goods knows what else is promised out of them and what is arriving; the branch asking does not.

## The steps

1. **Raise** — the branch says what it wants, from where, by when, and why. Nothing is committed.
2. **Submit** — it goes to whoever holds the stock.
3. **Agree or turn down** — they agree to all of it, part of it, or none of it, and say why.
4. **Transfer** — what was agreed becomes a transfer order, which is then shipped and received in the ordinary way.

## Nobody answers their own request

The person who raised a request cannot agree to it or turn it down. An approval you can give yourself is a checkbox.

The permissions follow the same line. Asking for stock and agreeing to send it are separate permissions, and agreeing to send it is the same permission as shipping it — because agreeing to a request *is* agreeing to ship.

## Less, never more

An answer may give less than was asked for. It may not give more.

Somebody who asked for ten and was sent twenty did not ask for twenty. A warehouse clearing its shelves into a branch that cannot sell them has moved a problem rather than solved one. If the branch should take more, it should ask for more.

## Nothing is reserved

Agreeing to a request does not hold the stock. The goods can still be sold from under it until the transfer is raised.

That is deliberate. An agreed request somebody forgot about would otherwise keep stock off the shelf indefinitely. The transfer order is what commits, so that is where the stock checks apply — and if the goods have gone in the meantime, that is where you find out.

## Sent once

Each line records how much has gone onto a transfer. What is agreed and not yet sent is the only thing a transfer can take, so a request cannot be sent twice. When everything agreed has gone, the request is fulfilled.

A line sent twice is stock nobody asked for arriving at a branch that cannot send it back without paperwork.

## Withdrawing

A request that has produced no transfer can be cancelled. Once anything has gone onto a transfer, it cannot — cancel or reverse the transfer instead, where the goods actually are.
