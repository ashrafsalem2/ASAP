# Stock transfers

A transfer moves stock from one location to another in two steps: shipping and receiving.

## Why two steps

Because for the days in between, the stock is in a lorry. Treating a transfer as a single instant would show it arrived the moment it left, and a shop counting its shelves would be short against a system that says otherwise.

Between the two, the stock sits in an in-transit location. It is on the company's books and on nobody's shelves, which is exactly true.

## Cost travels with the goods

The receiving location gets the cost the shipping location released, not a fresh valuation. A transfer is not a sale and does not make a profit; a shop that revalued incoming stock at its own average would book a gain or a loss for moving goods across a car park.

## Between branches

A transfer between two branches is one transaction with a side in each. The ledger entries carry the branch of the location they moved at, so a transfer out of Jeddah and into Riyadh reads correctly on both branches' figures rather than landing wholly on whichever one happened to be posted from.

## What is refused

Shipping more than is on hand, where the company does not allow negative stock. Receiving more than was shipped, always — the difference is a loss in transit and belongs on an adjustment, not hidden inside a transfer.
