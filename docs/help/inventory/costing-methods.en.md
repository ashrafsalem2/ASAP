# Costing methods

The costing method decides what a unit leaving the warehouse is worth. It is the single setting that most affects reported profit, and it cannot be changed once anything has been posted — not out of caution, but because it describes how the existing figures were calculated. Changing it would leave last year's cost of sales meaning something it never did.

## What ASAP supports

**FIFO.** The oldest cost leaves first. Inventory on hand is valued at the most recent purchases, which is closest to what it would cost to replace.

**Average.** Every receipt re-averages the cost of what is on hand. Smooth, undramatic, and the usual choice where units are interchangeable and prices move gently.

**Standard.** A cost set in advance, with the difference from what was actually paid booked as a variance. Tells you what a purchase cost against what you expected it to.

**Specific.** Each unit carries its own cost. For serialised goods where units are not interchangeable and a customer can point at the one they bought.

## What it does not decide

The method decides which cost leaves, not whether the cost is right. Where goods were sold before the purchase invoice arrived, the cost leaving is an estimate; the settlement routine corrects it afterwards, whichever method is in use. See cost adjustment.
