# Customer groups

A customer group is a kind of customer: wholesale, retail, staff, government. It is not an arrangement you have with somebody — it is what they are. That distinction is the whole point of the screen, and it is why a group lives with the customer while a price list does not.

Two other parts of the system were written against groups before there were any: an offer can be limited to a group, and a price list can be given to one. Until a customer is actually in a group, both of those match nobody.

## What a group is for

Setting the same thing a hundred times is how a hundred customers end up with ninety-eight of them right.

- **A price list for a whole class of customer.** Put every wholesaler on the trade list once, rather than assigning the list to each of them and then to each new one somebody adds next month.
- **An offer for a whole class of customer.** A staff discount is a group-limited offer. So is a promotion the retail counter runs and trade does not.

## Which price a customer actually pays

A customer's own price list beats their group's.

That is the same rule the prices inside a list follow — the more specific thing decides — and it is what lets you set a trade price for a hundred wholesalers while one of them keeps the arrangement somebody negotiated separately. If neither the customer nor their group is on a list, the price on the item applies.

Nothing here silently averages or combines. At each step there is one answer, and if there is not, the sale is refused rather than guessed at.

## Adding somebody to a group

Open the customer group screen, pick a group, and add the customer. A customer is in one group or none. There is no second group and no ordering to reason about, because a customer who was in two groups on two different price lists would be charged whichever the database read first.

## Switching a group off

A group is switched off, not deleted.

Once it is off, nobody new can be put in it. What does not happen is the customers already in it falling out: they stay where they are. This matters more than it sounds. A group that vanished would leave every offer and every price list that names it quietly matching nobody, and the first anyone would know of it is a customer being charged the counter price and nothing on the invoice explaining why.

If you genuinely want the members out, move them out first, and you will see what you are changing.

## When a group price stops applying

If a customer seems to be paying the wrong price, check in this order:

1. Is a price typed on the order line? A typed price beats everything.
2. Is the customer on a list of their own? Their own list beats their group's.
3. Is their group on a list, and is that list still live for the order date?
4. Is the customer in a group at all? A blank group is the usual answer.
