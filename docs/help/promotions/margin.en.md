# Margin protection

A campaign is planned weeks ahead and suppliers put their prices up in between. Margin protection is what stops that becoming a fortnight of selling below cost.

## Two checks, not one

**When the offer is saved**, every item it covers is priced at today's cost and anything below the floor is listed: the item, its cost, the offer price and the shortfall per unit. That is the moment somebody can act — the offer has not run yet, and the two items out of forty that ruin it are exactly the ones nobody would have thought to check. Saving is refused until it is dealt with.

**At every basket**, the same check runs again on what is actually being sold. Here it warns rather than refuses. A shop must not stop selling water because a promotion on it was misconfigured last week: the cashier cannot fix the offer, the customer is waiting, and refusing the sale punishes the wrong person for somebody else's mistake.

That asymmetry is deliberate. Refuse where somebody can act; warn where they cannot.

## The floor

The floor is a margin percentage set for the company. Zero means "never sell below cost". Ten means "never sell at less than ten per cent". Set it to what the business actually cannot afford rather than to what it would like, because a floor that refuses ordinary trading is a floor that gets overridden as a matter of routine and then means nothing.

## What it cannot see

The floor is checked against cost as recorded. Where a cost is still an estimate — goods sold before the purchase invoice arrived — the check is only as good as the estimate. Run cost adjustment before trusting a margin you intend to act on.
