# Bin movements

A bin movement records goods moved from one shelf to another inside one place. Restocking a pick face, consolidating two half-empty shelves, clearing a bay.

## What it does not change

Nothing is gained and nothing is lost. The quantity at the location is the same before and after, and so is what the stock is worth. Only the record of which shelf it is standing on moves.

This is why it is a document of its own rather than an adjustment out and an adjustment in. Recording it as a pair of adjustments would put both through costing — consuming a cost layer on the way out and creating a new one on the way in — fragmenting the layers and moving the valuation for a change that moved nothing but a box.

The entries it writes carry no value and take no part in costing. They sum to nothing at the location, so on-hand is untouched, and there are no value entries, so the valuation is untouched.

## One sheet, all or none

Somebody restocking moves eleven things at once. If one line is wrong, none of them post.

Posting ten of eleven is worse than posting none: the shelf and the record disagree, and nothing on either says which ten went through.

## What is refused

- **More than is on the shelf.** A bin movement never invents stock. If there is genuinely more there than the system says, that is a count, not a move.
- **Moving to the shelf it is already on.**
- **A shelf that is not there.** Bins are set up on the location, not created by whoever types one.
- **A place that does not track bins.** There is nothing to move between. Turn bins on for the location and count the stock onto its shelves first.

Two lines drawing on the same shelf are checked against what is left after the first, not against the untouched figure. Otherwise eleven lines could each take the whole shelf and the bin would go negative on posting.

## When a shelf disagrees with the record

Move what is genuinely there and count the difference. A bin movement says where goods went; a stock count says how many there are. Using one to do the other's job leaves the location's total wrong, and the location's total is what everything else is built on.
