# Employees

An employee record holds who somebody is, when they started, what they are paid and where they work. The last of those is a history rather than a field — see the branches topic for why.

## Numbers are never reused

An employee number is issued once and belongs to that person for good, including after they leave. Reusing a leaver's number attaches one person's service history to another, and service history is what an end-of-service award is calculated from. The number comes from a series and is taken on the day the record is created, not the day the person started.

## Leaving

Recording that somebody has left needs two things: the last day, and why. The reason is not a note about the departure — it is an input to what they are owed. Under the Labour Law a resignation is worth a fraction of a termination, and the fraction depends on how long they had been there. A leaving date with no reason cannot be costed, so it is refused.

Once somebody has left, the record stays. Last year's payroll points at it, and the end-of-service figure has to be defensible years later.

## Wages

Seeing the staff list and seeing what people are paid are separate permissions. A supervisor needs to know who works for them; far fewer people need to know what each of them earns. Where somebody may not see wages, the columns come back empty rather than as zero — zero would read as "unpaid", which is a different and defamatory claim.
