# General journals

A journal is a set of lines that must sum to zero. Nothing reaches the ledger any other way, which is why an unbalanced posting is impossible rather than merely discouraged.

## Preparing and posting are separate

Preparing a journal and committing it are two permissions on purpose. The person who keys a journal is usually not the person who approves it, and an ERP that treats them as one job has quietly removed a control the business thought it had.

## What a line must say

Every line names an account and an amount. The sign says the direction: positive debits, negative credits. A description is optional unless the company has turned on the setting that requires one — worth turning on where ledger enquiries are read by people who did not key the entries, because "1,200.00" against an account tells them nothing.

Where a line carries a tax code, the tax is worked out and its own lines are added before the balance is checked, so what you approve is what gets posted.

## Corrections

A posted journal is never edited. It is reversed: the mirror image is posted, the original stays visible, and the two point at each other. That is not caution for its own sake. An edited entry is one nobody can audit; a reversed one tells the whole story to somebody reading it next year.
